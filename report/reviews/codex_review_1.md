# Codex adversarial review 1 (gpt-6-astra, high) — 2026-09-10

Scope: report Secs. 2-4, code/recon_noise.py, three_tracer.py, cross_spectrum.py, forest_power.py, mathematica/*.wls, PLAN.md Sec. 6 checklist. Verbatim output follows.

---

The continuum quadratic-estimator normalization is sound. I found no missing factor of two in `eq:Nkappa`, `fisher_slices`, or `_kpar_sum`. The more serious problems concern the extension to a broad redshift slab, the interpretation of deprojection, and the proposed measurement's response calibration.

I reran the Mathematica checks in isolated states, reproduced the DR1 ACT forecast S/N=2.0736, and checked numerical convergence without modifying files. Refining the reconstruction integral changed N_kappa(100) from 9.6287e-6 to 9.6494e-6: about 0.2%.

For the independent white-spectrum check, let Δk = k_par,max − k_par,min. The number of **real** degrees of freedom per steradian is (D Δk/π)(l_max²/4π). Each contributes Fisher information 2, giving N_kappa^-1 = D Δk l_max²/(2π²). Positive radial frequencies contain two real screens (sine and cosine), so the positive-k_par doubling does not double-count reality-conjugate modes. At finite L, replace π l_max² by the overlap area of the two allowed Fourier disks. For the existing white test, that gives 4.899913e6; the implementation gives 4.885298e6, converging to within 0.04% on a finer grid. The mode coupling, squeezed dilation/shear coefficients, and discrete Gaussian Fisher factors also check out.

1. **Significant — The cubic weights omit the stated potential-to-convergence conversion.** report/main.tex:148, equations `eq:E` and `eq:cubic`. E^mu and b_mu are defined for coefficients of phi. The cubic expression subsequently uses them directly for kappa-hat. As written, its explicit W_ijk needs E^{kappa,L} = (L²/2) E^{phi,L}, b^{kappa,L} = (L²/2) b^{phi,L}. Also, a masked harmonic transform cannot acquire diagonal covariance simply through normalization. The stated unbiased bandpower expectation requires a survey-window response/mixing matrix. **Fix:** distinguish the periodic-patch derivation from the masked estimator and include both conversions explicitly.

2. **Significant — The claimed second-order "N^(1)-type" cross bias is incorrectly derived.** report/main.tex:168. For Gaussian lensing fields and an independent Gaussian forest, the proposed second-order contribution contains <phi phi kappa_CMB> = 0. Pairing the remaining phi with "the two delta_F fields" is not a Gaussian Wick contraction. Gaussian lensing corrections to the cross-response instead appear at third order in the remapping expansion, schematically C^{phi phi} C^{phi kappa_CMB}. A second-order term can arise from a nonzero lensing bispectrum or additional non-Gaussian correlations, but requires a different derivation. **Fix:** replace this paragraph with the actual perturbative terms; remove the unsupported "few-per-cent" estimate.

3. **Significant — The reported intrinsic-response contamination has a factor-of-two normalization error.** code/three_tracer.py:26, lines 149 and 187; report/main.tex:367. `R_FID` is defined as dlnP_F/ddelta_L. However, `R_ka` responds to the **field** modulation delta_F -> (1+a) delta_F, for which dlnP_F = 2a. For the assumed constant isotropic power response, kappa-hat = kappa_Lya + (R_FID/2) R_ka delta_s + n. The code uses R_FID R_ka. Thus the DR1 L=100 bias/signal entry should be approximately **1.57 rather than 3.14**, within this response model. The claimed ~7 sigma response detection also needs an actual calculation: the "naive" S/N column uses only the lensing signal in its numerator. **Fix:** normalize the density-response kernel directly and recompute contamination and response-detection significance.

4. **Blocker — One broad-slab quasar map does not generally cancel arbitrary linear forest responses.** report/main.tex:283, `eq:pmodel`–`eq:deproj`; PLAN.md:15. The identity is correct **conditional on** p_alpha = T_alpha kappa_Lya + R_alpha delta_s. The proposed measurement generally has p_alpha^int = ∫ dchi A_alpha(chi) delta(chi), with short-bin-dependent weights from forest evolution, coverage, noise, and continuum fitting. Its residual is ∫ dchi A_alpha(chi) [W_CMB(chi) − beta_L b_q(chi) W_q(chi)] P((L+1/2)/chi, chi)/chi². Cancellation for **arbitrary** A_alpha requires the bracket to vanish pointwise, not merely after averaging with one fiducial slab kernel. This is not negligible by construction: the implemented W_CMB increases from 3.605e-4 to 4.858e-4 h/Mpc across the slab. Near-constancy of beta_L with L does not establish constancy with redshift. The tidal claim is valid for an exactly transverse long mode. But projection through a finite radial window does not select only k_par=0. Density, tides, and quasar RSD then have different radial transfer functions. The Mathematica check verifies the transverse special case only. **Fix:** use narrower tomographic bins or redshift-weighted quasar templates whose biased kernel matches the CMB kernel; test the remaining radial/tidal transfer mismatch explicitly. Replace "anything linear in the long mode cancels" with the actual kernel-matching condition.

5. **Significant — Magnification is included in the spectra but inconsistently treated in beta; residuals are first order.** code/three_tracer.py:114; report/main.tex:319. Within the code's model, the cancellation coefficient is beta_cancel = C^{d kc}/(b_q C^{dd} + m C^{d kLya}). The code omits the magnification term in this denominator, while retaining it elsewhere. At L=100, the implemented, exact-model, and observable-ratio values are respectively 0.084016, 0.083928, C^{q kc}/C^{qq} = 0.090999. The implementation mismatch is small here; treating the observable ratio as the cancellation coefficient is substantially worse. An error delta-beta leaves −delta-beta R_alpha C^{dq}, **first order** in the coefficient error. The subsection's "second order in imperfections" characterization is therefore misleading. Matter inside the slab also lenses its more distant forest pixels. This does **not** invalidate the algebra if C^{kLya q} is retained, but it removes real lensing signal. The code already includes this intrinsic overlap. Its total signal subtraction at L=100 is **3.74%**, including **1.19%** from intrinsic slab overlap, rather than the text's ~1.5%. **Fix:** fit magnification and bias consistently, use the actual quasar source distribution for kappa_q, and retain the intrinsic overlap in the signal template.

6. **Significant — Estimating beta from these data has an unforecasted statistical cost.** report/main.tex:319; PLAN.md:45. All tabulated forecasts treat beta_L as known. The proposed empirical ratio has a noisy, shot-subtracted denominator and is correlated with both bispectra entering D. Using the repo's Gaussian spectra, ACT noise, f_sky=0.15, and ΔL=30, error propagation including numerator–denominator covariance gives approximately **31% fractional uncertainty at L=100** and **49% at L=40** for that ratio. These are diagnostic bin errors, not a forecast for a joint smooth fit. **Fix:** forecast the joint spectra/bispectra likelihood with b_q(z), magnification, and beta uncertainty. Do not insert noisy per-bin ratios into a fixed-beta covariance.

7. **Significant — The proposed "quasars behind the slab" remedy changes the sample drastically.** report/main.tex:324; PLAN.md:79. Taking forest sources behind the entire 2.1<z<3.0 slab means z_q>3. For the standard 1040–1205 Å rest-frame forest region, this implies z_pix >= 1040(1+3)/1215.67 − 1 ≈ 2.42. It therefore eliminates standard-region coverage of the slab's lower portion. Being behind an individual absorber does not make a source independent of the **whole slab's** quasar map. Pure geometry-dependent mean fields can be removed by correct conditional subtraction. But merely using actual positions does not establish that flux selection, correlated noise, shared-source terms, and magnification-induced selection leave no residual response. **Fix:** use narrow slabs with disjoint source/template selections, or model the shared sampling explicitly; recompute coverage and noise after the selection.

8. **Significant — The deprojected variance is wrong in the report and incomplete in the code.** report/main.tex:342; code/three_tracer.py:116. The code correctly computes C_perp = C_cc + N_c − 2 beta C_cq + beta² (C_qq + N_q). The report's expression involving C_cc(1−r²) plus another full quasar-power term is not equivalent for its stated r. At L=100, it gives 4.093e-7, versus the code's 3.670e-7. More fundamentally, deprojection removes the intrinsic response from the **mean cross-spectrum**, not from the forest reconstruction's auto-power. With X = kappa_Lya + a delta_s + n, C_XX = C_kk + 2a C_kd + a² C_dd + N_X. The code omits the last two signal terms. Including the constant-response contribution with the corrected factor of two lowers the full-range toy ACT S/N only from 2.074 to about **2.056**; it is small here, but the variance formula is still incomplete. **Fix:** use the full joint covariance, including response sample variance, connected forest terms, and coefficient uncertainty.

9. **Significant — The Poisson noise formula is reasonable; its use as a complete sparse-QE covariance is unverified.** report/main.tex:329; code/recon_noise.py:55. For independent Poisson sightlines, the diagonal sampled-field covariance does contain the **full** P_1D(k_par) = ∫ d²k_perp/(2π)² P_F, not only power above a transverse cutoff. Including it alongside resolved P_F is not double-counting: the terms arise from same-sightline and distinct-sightline contributions. The unchanged lensing response is also correct under the single-plane, fixed-selection assumptions. What is unverified is replacing the full sampling covariance by independent Gaussian modes with this diagonal power. Sampling induces additional mode covariances; McQuinn & White explicitly discuss them. Poisson sampling also has no sharp Nyquist frequency π sqrt(nbar). Keeping that cutoff after adding aliasing is an additional restriction, not another noise contribution. Holding density/noise fixed and changing l_max from 900 to 1800 lowers N_kappa(100) from 9.64e-6 to 7.87e-6. **Fix:** validate the approximation against the discrete estimator on realistic geometries and demonstrate cutoff stability. Specify whether same-sightline products are excluded: if retained, a physical amplitude modulation also modulates the aliasing power, which the current `fa=P1+P2` does not include.

10. **Significant — Harmonic weighting is implemented correctly, but density definitions and sample inputs are not consistent enough for a DR1 forecast.** code/three_tracer.py:71; report/main.tex:334. Minimizing N = <w²(P_N+P_1D)>/(nbar_los <w>²) gives w ∝ (P_N+P_1D)^-1 and the stated harmonic mean. The Gauss–Hermite implementation is correct. However, the prefactor must be the **unweighted available-sightline density**. Conventionally, a noise-weighted n_eff = nbar_los <P_1D/(P_N+P_1D)> already incorporates pixel noise. Using that definition in the current formula would count weighting twice. The report's own length arithmetic gives 45×350/712 = 22.1, not 25 sightlines/deg². Substituting 22.1 raises N_kappa(100) by about **25%**. Neither average-density replacement captures the nonlinear dependence on actual n(z), pair overlap, and noise. I reproduced P_1D(0.1) = 0.1938 h^-1 Mpc, with a converged transverse integral. Its observational calibration remains unverified; the approximate forest amplitude matters once pixel noise is present. **Fix:** distinguish nbar_los from weighted n_eff, use measured joint coverage/noise distributions, and forecast the actual pixel/pair weighting rather than equating it to full C^-1.

11. **Significant — Both Limber implementations truncate the CMB auto-spectrum at z=6.** code/cross_spectrum.py:22; code/three_tracer.py:42. The distance, power, H_0/c, and slab-kernel units are consistent: I found no missing h, chi², or Δchi factor. But the common integration cap excludes real CMB lensing power. An independent linear-theory integration above z=6 adds approximately **7%, 15%, 24%, and 32%** of the currently computed C_cc at L=40, 100, 300, 763. Forest–CMB signal spectra are unaffected by this particular cutoff. **Fix:** integrate the CMB auto-spectrum to recombination, or use a validated CAMB convergence spectrum. Then redo the white-noise calibration. Separately validate non-Limber slab spectra at the lowest multipoles.

12. **Significant — Matching published auto-spectrum S/N does not validate the CMB noise or the measurement's multipole range.** code/cross_spectrum.py:40; code/three_tracer.py:38; PLAN.md:46. The quoted calibration targets are reasonable: ACT reports 43 sigma, and Planck PR4's amplitude uncertainty is approximately 0.024. But one integrated constraint cannot determine N_L where this cross-correlation gets its information. The table sums 2<=L<=3000, including low-L extrapolation of the reconstruction noise, whereas the plan uses approximately 30<=L<=300. Keeping all other assumptions unchanged, the ACT DR1 result becomes **1.72 for 40<=L<=300**. The released ACT map contains lower modes, but their reliability is application-dependent; their inclusion needs validation. **Fix:** use released N_L, filters, actual overlap masks, and the pipeline's angular transfer functions. ACT also specifies a squared-mask treatment and simulation-derived residual response for cross-correlations; generic apodization plus MASTER is insufficient by itself. See the ACT map instructions on LAMBDA.

13. **Blocker — The real-space template is exact for a locally affine lens, not for arbitrary lensing at linear order.** PLAN.md:10; report/main.tex:121. For constant kappa, gamma_1, gamma_2 across a pair, delta-xi = −r_perp xi_{,r_perp} [kappa + gamma_1 cos2phi + gamma_2 sin2phi] is correct, including identical radial templates. Sparse sampling does not invalidate this pair-level statement. But the exact first-order response is grad xi · [alpha(theta_a) − alpha(theta_b)]. For a lens plane wave, its ratio to the local-gradient approximation contains sinc(L·theta_ab/2). At L=300, r_perp=30 h^-1 Mpc, and a pair aligned with L, this is **0.798**. The discrepancy is already linear in lensing amplitude. Finite cells add another angular window. **Fix:** use exact pair responses or calibrate the scale-dependent response matrix with injections. Alternatively impose and verify L r_perp/chi << 1. The current "exact at linear order" claim is too strong.

14. **Significant — Differentiating the measured distorted xi-bar is not automatically the correct lensing response.** PLAN.md:77. If continuum fitting applies a projection P, the response is C^{obs}_{,kappa} = P C_{,kappa} P^T for fixed P. It equals a transverse derivative of the measured mean only when projection, binning, geometry averaging, and differentiation commute. That can hold in restricted homogeneous limits; varying forest lengths, weights, pair redshifts, and source planes prevent assuming it generally. DESI explicitly propagates the continuum projection through a distortion matrix in its correlation model. **Fix:** propagate lensing derivatives through the same projection/binning operators, or demonstrate equivalence with injections before using the measured derivative as the normalization.

15. **Significant — The stored geometry sums cannot generally recover unbiased shear; E/B needs a window treatment.** PLAN.md:43. Fitting basis functions h=(1, cos2phi, sin2phi) requires G_ab = sum w_i w_j h_a h_b. Besides W, W_2c, W_2s, this needs fourth-harmonic information, because cos²2phi = (1+cos4phi)/2, sin2phi cos2phi = sin4phi/2. Those sums are absent from the proposed outputs. Sparse cells can also have nearly singular angular coverage. Linear scalar lensing and transverse tides are E-type in the ideal geometry. Masks and sampling mix recovered E/B, and a passing B×kappa test cannot rule out an E-type intrinsic contaminant. **Fix:** store the full angular normal matrix, handle ill-conditioned cells, assign boundary-crossing pairs uniquely, and calibrate spin conventions and E/B leakage through the actual footprint.

16. **Significant — Shifting sightlines by −alpha is a valid restricted mock construction, not full pipeline validation.** PLAN.md:20, line 49. The sign is correct when existing samples represent source coordinates: theta_obs = theta_src − alpha(theta_src) + O(alpha²). Conversely, mocks conditioned on fixed observed positions require sampling the field at theta_obs + alpha(theta_obs). Relabelling existing skewers changes the sampling pattern and boundary selection. A single shift per skewer also enforces the single-source-plane approximation; it cannot reproduce redshift-dependent deflection along the forest. These mocks will not test broad-slab deprojection unless forest responses, quasars, foreground density, and both convergences have the correct joint correlations. **Fix:** retain the shift method as a sign/response test, and add fixed-observed-geometry mocks sampled from a volume with redshift-dependent deflections and the actual selection/noise/continuum operations.

17. **Significant — The proposed covariance/null tests do not yet validate the deprojected statistic.** PLAN.md:47, lines 69–72. Independent CMB simulations crossed with fixed forest/quasar data do not reproduce their shared intrinsic response or coefficient-estimation covariance. With fixed beta, replacing only the CMB map leaves the real subtraction −beta C^{Xq} in every realization. Such simulations are useful conditional diagnostics, but are not automatically a null ensemble for the physical deprojection. Ten to twenty joint mocks are useful for gross recovery tests, but provide a noisy covariance and cannot support inversion for more than 9–19 independent components. Likewise, a 150-region jackknife requires attention to covariance dimension and long-mode correlations. **Fix:** move injection/recovery and joint-covariance validation into phase 1, before interpreting an amplitude. Refit beta and other estimated quantities in the appropriate mock likelihood, and demonstrate covariance stability.

18. **Minor — The pair-count scale is plausible; the throughput justification is arithmetically inconsistent.** PLAN.md:55. At z=2.4, a 30 h^-1 Mpc transverse radius covers approximately 0.593 deg². At 25 active sightlines/deg², that gives about 14.8 neighboring sightlines. With 1.7e8 pixels and 0.549 h^-1 Mpc spacing, a simple unique-pair estimate is 1.38e11. Thus 3e11 candidate operations is a plausible order of magnitude. However, 3e11/(24×1e8) = 125 seconds, not 1–3 hours. The stated runtime instead implies approximately 1.2–3.5e6 pairs/s/thread. Histogram updates, memory access, masks, and thread scaling need measurement. **Fix:** benchmark the actual accumulation kernel on a representative patch and extrapolate counted candidates and accepted pairs separately. The machine may be sufficient; the current throughput argument does not establish the runtime.

**Overall verdict:** Table 2 is reproducible as an optimistic Gaussian toy forecast. It is not yet a credible forecast of a **clean 2 sigma DR1 measurement**. The continuum normalization survives review, but broad-slab response cancellation and the cell estimator's normalization remain unvalidated. The same toy model already gives roughly 1.7 sigma over a conservative version of the proposed measurement range, before several measurement losses. A marginal detection remains plausible; the present report overstates how securely its significance and cleanliness have been established.
