\title{Weak lensing of the DESI DR1 Lyman-$\alpha$ forest by low-redshift galaxies and quasars:\\ estimator, validation and a first amplitude constraint}
\author{An\v{z}e Slosar (with Claude)}
\date{18 September 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Gravitational lensing by structure at $z<2$ displaces the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest sightlines coherently, which dilates and shears the transverse correlation of the forest flux. We measure this effect with a pair-based quadratic estimator: the products of forest pixels on pairs of sightlines are weighted by the response of the measured forest correlation to a deflection and projected on a template of the lensing deflection built from DESI DR1 galaxies and quasars (BGS, LRG, ELG and QSO samples) and BOSS DR12 galaxies at $0.1<z<1.6$ in five redshift slices. The tracer template shares no density modes with the forest at $2.1<z<3$, so unlike a CMB-lensing template it is free of the intrinsic forest--density three-point term that we found to bias the CMB cross-correlation at the $0.4$ level of the lensing amplitude. On 400 self-lensed mock realisations with lognormal tracers the estimator is unbiased (null $-0.05\pm0.08$, recovery $0.91\pm0.08$, paired response $0.966\pm0.025$ for a true amplitude of one). The forest correlation that sets the response kernel is fitted with explicit redshift evolution ($b_F\propto(1+z)^{3.5}$), which accounts for half of the amplitude difference between pixel pairs in the Ly$\alpha$ and Ly$\beta$ rest-frame windows. The tracer biases are measured from NaMaster auto-spectra and the deflection templates themselves are validated against ACT DR6 and Planck PR4 CMB lensing: relative to the prediction for the template, the deflection cross-spectrum has amplitude $0.99\pm0.04$ (ACT) and $0.92\pm0.03$ (Planck) over $L\le500$, and $0.96\pm0.04$ and $0.92\pm0.03$ over the fiducial window $L\le1000$. Applied to every DR1 forest pixel below $z=3$ (427\,888 sightlines with $1.96\le z\le3.0$, effective redshift $z_{\rm eff}=2.35$, over $11\,000\,{\rm deg}^2$, both delta regions), the forest auto-correlation gives a lensing amplitude relative to the $\Lambda$CDM prediction of $A=0.35\pm0.47$ (single slab; Fisher scale 0.38), with curl, random-template and injection tests consistent with no systematic. The same estimator applied to the quasar--forest cross-correlation, with its own fitted correlation model (quasar bias, redshift offset and smoothing), gives an independent $A=0.61\pm0.52$, and the optimal combination with the fiducial template band set $40\le L\le1000$, across which the templates are validated against CMB lensing, is $A=0.58\pm0.31$ with per-slice response matrices, and $A=0.61\pm0.33$, i.e. $A<1.16$ at 95 per cent, in the adopted fiducial with Wiener weights from the measured tracer covariance and one joint $55\times55$ response matrix over all slice templates (auto $0.42\pm0.43$, cross $0.86\pm0.47$); the narrower $40$--$500$ set of the mock validation gives $0.47\pm0.35$ and a convergence test to $L=1300$ gives $0.65\pm0.31$ with no gain in precision. Injecting the template deflection into the data is recovered with slope $1.06$ (auto) and $1.00$ (cross). A detection at this depth was not expected; the full DESI sample should reach $2$--$3\sigma$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Forest.} The DESI DR1 Lyman-$\alpha$ forest deltas \citep{2503.14745,2306.06312}, extracted with \texttt{picca} v9 on a 0.8\,\AA\ grid with the lines, DLA (combined CNN + Gaussian-process catalogue, $N_{\rm HI}>10^{20.3}\,{\rm cm}^{-2}$) and BAL masks applied by the DR1 extraction. The catalogue provides two rest-frame windows and we use both: region A, $1040$--$1205$\,\AA, the Ly$\alpha$ forest proper, and region B, $920$--$1020$\,\AA, blueward of the Ly$\beta$ emission line. A region-B pixel at observed wavelength $\lambda$ carries Ly$\alpha$ absorption at $z=\lambda/1215.67-1$, which is the field we want, and simultaneously Ly$\beta$ absorption at $z=\lambda/1025.72-1$; over our slab the second sits at $z=2.7$--$3.7$, more than $1000\Mpch$ further away. A pixel pair inside $30\Mpch$ that joins an A and a B pixel therefore measures Ly$\alpha$--Ly$\alpha$ alone, while a B--B pair also carries Ly$\beta$--Ly$\beta$ at a common redshift; we use A$\times$A and A$\times$B pairs and drop B$\times$B. Region B is treated as an extension of the same sightline: the two segments are concatenated and ordered in distance, each pixel carrying a region tag, and each segment keeps its own continuum-fit block (Section~\ref{sec:qe}).

We keep every pixel of the DR1 grid below $z=3.0$: the grid starts at 3600\,\AA, i.e.\ $z=1.96$, in both windows, so the slab is $1.96\le z\le3.0$ (the first version of this note cut at $z=2.1$, a limit inherited from the mock box that discarded 16 per cent of the region-A pixel weight and 22 per cent of the region-B one). Per region, forests with at least 50 such pixels are kept: 427\,888 sightlines, $2.80\times10^8$ pixels of which 19.5 per cent are in region B, 11\,000\,deg$^2$. The weighted mean pixel redshift, which we adopt as the effective forest redshift and as the source plane of the templates, is $z_{\rm eff}=2.348$ (unweighted mean 2.342, median 2.292; the $2.1$--$3.0$ sample had $z_{\rm eff}=2.416$). Pixel weights are the \texttt{picca} weights $w=1/(\eta\sigma_N^2+\sigma^2_{\rm LSS}(\lambda))$ with $\eta\simeq1$ and $\sigma^2_{\rm LSS}=0.07$--$0.19$: the diagonal of the inverse covariance, including the signal variance. The per-forest noise power $P_N=\sigma_N^2\Delta\chi_{\rm pix}$ has median $0.54\Mpch$ and a scatter of 2.2 in $\ln P_N$.

\paragraph{Tracers.} Three catalogues. (i) The DESI DR1 large-scale-structure clustering catalogues v1.5 \citep{2405.16593}: LRG ($0.4<z<1.1$), ELG\_LOPnotqso ($0.8<z<1.6$), QSO ($z<1.75$ here) and the magnitude-limited bright-galaxy sample BGS\_BRIGHT$-21.5$ ($0.1<z<0.4$), with their completeness, redshift-failure and imaging-systematics weights, and two random catalogues per Galactic cap (5000\,deg$^{-2}$) carrying redshifts and weights. (ii) The BOSS DR12 combined LOWZ+CMASS galaxy sample \citep[CMASSLOWZTOT;][]{2016MNRAS.455.1553R}, $0.1<z<0.8$, with the weight $w_{\rm systot}(w_{\rm cp}+w_{\rm noz}-1)$ and one random catalogue per cap; BOSS covers sky that DR1 does not (and vice versa), which is why it is included. They are split into five redshift slices, $0.1$--$0.4$ (BGS, BOSS), $0.4$--$0.6$ (LRG, BOSS), $0.6$--$0.8$ (LRG, BOSS), $0.8$--$1.1$ (LRG, ELG, QSO) and $1.1$--$1.6$ (ELG, QSO), ending at $z=1.6$ so that the tracers stay $400\Mpch$ in front of a forest that now starts at $z=1.96$ (the $1.6$--$1.75$ quasar slice of the first version held 1--2 per cent of the convergence power and is dropped). Figure~\ref{fig:kernel} shows where they sit under the lensing kernel of a source at $z_{\rm eff}=2.35$: the five slices hold about 95 per cent of the power of $\kl$, the $0.1$--$0.4$ slice alone 19--29 per cent.

\paragraph{CMB lensing.} The ACT DR6 baseline convergence \citep{2304.05203,2304.05202} and the Planck PR4 convergence \citep[the ``2018-like'' minimum-variance reconstruction of][rotated from Galactic to equatorial coordinates]{2022JCAP...09..039C} are used to validate the tracer biases and the deflection templates (Section~\ref{sec:templates}).

\section{Estimator}
\label{sec:estimator}
\subsection{Lensing of pair correlations}
Let $\dF(\vth,\chi)$ be the forest flux fluctuation observed along a sightline at angular position $\vth$ and comoving distance $\chi$. Lensing maps the observed position to the true one, $\vth_{\rm true}=\vth+\bm\alpha(\vth)$, with $\bm\alpha=\nabla\phi$ and $\kappa=-\nabla^2\phi/2$, so that the observed field is the true field sampled at $\vth+\bm\alpha$. For a pair of sightlines $a,b$ the correlation of two pixels at transverse separation $r_\perp=\chi|\vth_a-\vth_b|$ and radial separation $r_\parallel$ is the true correlation at the displaced separation,
\begin{equation}
\langle\dF(a,\chi_p)\dF(b,\chi_q)\rangle=\xi_F\!\left(\bigl|\vth_{ab}+\bm\alpha_a-\bm\alpha_b\bigr|\chi,\,r_\parallel\right)
\simeq\xi_F(r_\perp,r_\parallel)+\frac{\partial\xi_F}{\partial r_\perp}\,\chi\,\hat\vth_{ab}\cdot(\bm\alpha_a-\bm\alpha_b),
\label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}
with $\hat\vth_{ab}$ the unit pair direction (from $b$ to $a$). For a pair separation small compared with the wavelength of the lens, $\bm\alpha_a-\bm\alpha_b\simeq(\vth_{ab}\cdot\nabla)\nabla\phi$: the convergence dilates the transverse correlation isotropically and the shear makes it anisotropic. Equation~\eqref{eq:pair} is exact in the pair displacement and does not require the squeezed limit; the deflection difference is evaluated from the template at the two sightlines.

\subsection{Quadratic estimator with a deflection template}
\label{sec:qe}
Given a template deflection $\bm\alpha^T(\vth)$ for the lensing field, the amplitude $A$ of the deflection in the data ($\bm\alpha=A\,\bm\alpha^T$ in expectation) is estimated from all pixel pairs on all sightline pairs with $3\le r_\perp\le30\Mpch$ and $|r_\parallel|<30\Mpch$,
\begin{equation}
\hat A=F^{-1}(q-\bar q),\qquad
q=\sum_{ab}\;\bigl[\chi_{ab}\,\hat\vth_{ab}\cdot(\bm\alpha^T_a-\bm\alpha^T_b)\bigr]\sum_{p\in a,q\in b}w_pw_q\,\dF(p)\dF(q)\,\xi'_F(r_\perp,r_\parallel),
\label{eq:qe}
\end{equation}
with $\xi'_F=\partial\xi_F/\partial r_\perp$ the response kernel, $\bar q$ the mean field (the same sum with $\dF(p)\dF(q)$ replaced by $\xi_F$: it removes the correlation of the sightline density with the template and is not zero on a masked footprint), and $F$ the response matrix, $\sum w_pw_q\,\xi'^2_F\,[\chi\hat\vth\cdot\Delta\bm\alpha^T]^2$ up to the same geometric factors. This is the optimal quadratic estimator for the pixel-diagonal inverse covariance; a full per-sightline inverse covariance would shape the weights along $k_\parallel$ and we estimate its gain at 8 per cent in signal-to-noise, not worth its complexity here. The pair geometry uses a local tangent-plane metric per pair, valid at the separations used; the sightline density is 19--22\,deg$^{-2}$ so the pair count is $5.1\times10^6$. A pixel pair is skipped when both of its pixels are in region B.

The template deflection is split into five multipole bands, $40\le L<100$, $100$--$200$, $200$--$300$, $300$--$400$ and $400$--$500$ (cosine-tapered windows), plus the complementary ``junk'' band, and each band has a curl partner (the deflection rotated by $90^\circ$). The tracer auto-spectra follow linear theory to $\ell\simeq600$ (Figure~\ref{fig:spectra}) and the template alm reaches $\ell=1000$, so the two bands above 300 carry signal; the deflection is evaluated at nside 1024 to resolve the top band. The eleven components are fitted jointly with the $11\times11$ response matrix; the science amplitude is the common amplitude of the three gradient bands with the curl and junk amplitudes marginalised (a ``curl null'' is reported as the mean curl amplitude). Errors are from a HEALPix jackknife on the pair midpoints (nside 8, 301 regions on DR1) and are checked against the Fisher scale $F^{-1/2}$ and against random templates.

\paragraph{The response kernel.} $\xi_F(r_\perp,r_\parallel,z)$ is measured from the same forests in 1\,$\Mpch$ cells, split by the pair mean redshift $z$ (seven bins between 1.96 and 3.0, with the pair-weighted mean redshift of every cell recorded), and fitted with one evolving model. With $x\equiv(1+z)/(1+z_{\rm ref})$, $z_{\rm ref}=2.4$,
\begin{multline}
\xi_F(r_\perp,r_\parallel,z)=b_F^2\Big[\frac{D(z)}{D(z_{\rm ref})}\Big]^2x^{2\gamma_b}\,\big[T_0+2\beta_F(z)\,T_1+\beta_F(z)^2\,T_2\big](r_\perp,r_\parallel)\\
+x^{\gamma_S}\sigma(r)\,S(r_\perp,r_\parallel)+N(r_\perp)\,\mathbb{1}[\,|r_\parallel|<1\Mpch\,],\qquad\beta_F(z)=\beta_F\,x^{\gamma_\beta},
\label{eq:xifit}
\end{multline}
where $T_{0,1,2}$ are the projected basis tables of the $\mu^0$, $\mu^2$ and $\mu^4$ terms of the linear model $P_F(\bm k)=b_F^2(1+\beta_F\mu^2)^2P(k,z_{\rm ref})$ pushed through the DESI pixel and resolution windows and the per-forest continuum operator, $D$ is the linear growth factor, $(\gamma_b,\gamma_\beta,\gamma_S)$ are the power-law indices of the bias, of $\beta_F$ and of the correction (three parameters; the same-wavelength term $N$ is instrumental and not evolved); $S$ is a tensor-product cubic B-spline in $(r_\perp,r_\parallel)$ carried on a strictly positive envelope $\sigma(r)$, $r=(r_\perp^2+r_\parallel^2)^{1/2}$ (the monopole of the reference shape), which removes the dynamic range of $\xi_F$ from the spline coefficients; and $N$ is a one-dimensional spline confined to the first radial bin. Every basis element is averaged over the 1\,$\Mpch$ cell exactly as the pair counts are binned, and the parameters are fitted by weighted least squares with the accumulated pair weights (a variable-projection least squares: the spline coefficients are solved exactly at every step of the non-linear fit), so that $\xi_F$ and its \emph{analytic} derivative $\xi'_F$ follow the data at every redshift rather than a two-parameter shape; the fitted table is layered in comoving distance (21 nodes over $2.05<z<3.05$) and the pair kernel interpolates between the two layers bracketing each pair's mean distance. Three points matter.

\emph{(i) Why the shape has to be fitted.} The estimator normalisation is $\langle\hat A\rangle=A\,\langle Wg_{\rm used}g_{\rm true}\rangle/\langle Wg_{\rm used}^2\rangle$ with $g=\chi\,\xi'_F$ and $W$ the pair weight, so an error in the \emph{shape} of the kernel biases the amplitude at first order; it does not cancel. The two-parameter table misses the DR1 measurement by 5--12 per cent at $r_\parallel<5\Mpch$, which is where 80 per cent of the information is, and its free three-coefficient version returns $\beta_F=0.43$ from the $\mu^2$ term against $\beta_F=1.76$ from the $\mu^4$ term: the Kaiser form itself is rejected. Fitting the correction changes $\chi^2$ from 1880 to 235 over 810 cells and raises the amplitude normalisation by 8 per cent.

\emph{(ii) The continuum projection.} The correlation the pixels see is $P_aCP_b^\mathsf{T}$ with $P_a$ the mean-and-slope removal of the continuum fit on forest $a$; it is the largest single distortion of the model. Earlier iterations evaluated it for one representative forest spanning the whole slab (1295 pixels, $712\Mpch$) with uniform weights, while the median picca forest has 689 pixels ($397\Mpch$); that over-predicted $\xi_F$ by 5--20 per cent, growing with $r_\perp$. It is now the pair-weight average of $P_aCP_b^\mathsf{T}$ over sampled pairs of real DR1 forests, with the measured pixel weights and with the projector running over every pixel the continuum fit saw (not only the pixels inside $1.96<z<3.0$). The corrected projection removes the mean offset but predicts more $r_\perp$ tilt than the data show, which is why the empirical correction is still needed.

\emph{(iii) The same-wavelength term.} Pixels at the same observed wavelength on different sightlines carry an extra positive correlation of $\simeq10^{-3}$, flat in $r_\perp$ beyond $6\Mpch$ and significant at 5--7$\sigma$ per cell, absent from the neighbouring radial bins (Figure~\ref{fig:xicorr}): a sky-subtraction and calibration residual, not a correlation of the density field. It therefore belongs in the mean field, which subtracts $\langle\dF\dF\rangle$, but carries no lensing response, and $N$ is excluded from $\xi'_F$ for that reason.

On DR1 the fit gives $b_F^2=0.0296$ and $\beta_F=1.04$ over $3\le r_\perp<30$, $r_\parallel<30\Mpch$ (Figure~\ref{fig:xi}); the base parameters are not measurements of the forest bias, since the correction absorbs part of the shape.

\emph{(iv) Redshift evolution, and the two pair types.} A$\times$A and A$\times$B pairs do not have the same correlation: fitted separately with one non-evolving model they give $b_F^2=0.0302$ against $0.0253$, an amplitude ratio of $0.84$. The A$\times$B pairs sit at lower redshift (a region-B pixel is bluer than any region-A pixel of the same quasar), so the natural reading is redshift evolution of the forest correlation. The measured cells are therefore split by the pair mean redshift (seven bins between $z=1.96$ and $3.0$, with the pair-weighted mean redshift of every cell recorded) and one evolving model, Equation~\eqref{eq:xifit}, is fitted to all of them.
On DR1 the base model alone gives $b_F^2=0.032$, $\beta_F=1.03$, $\gamma_b=3.83$, $\gamma_\beta=-2.24$, i.e.\ $b_F\propto(1+z)^{3.8}$ and a total amplitude exponent ${\rm d}\ln(b_F^2D^2)/{\rm d}\ln(1+z)=5.7$ (on the $2.1$--$3.0$ sample of the previous version: $3.58$, $-1.64$ and $5.2$); with the correction included, $\gamma_b=3.84$, $\gamma_\beta=-2.07$, $\gamma_S=6.2$, with $\chi^2=1733$ over the 5670 cells, i.e.\ 248 per redshift bin of 810 cells against 239 for the non-evolving fit on the collapsed $2.1$--$3.0$ cells, so the power laws describe every bin, the new lowest one included. Since the correction evolves like the base amplitude, the split between the two is degenerate in that fit and only their sum, the kernel, is determined. Refitting the two pair types with the exponents held fixed gives an A$\times$B to A$\times$A amplitude ratio of $0.92$ at fixed redshift: evolution explains half of the difference, and the remaining 8 per cent is a property of the region-B pairs that the single evolving table does not model (\texttt{report/stageb/xi\_zevol\_dr1.json}). Non-linear Arinyo-i-Prats terms were tested and make the fit \emph{worse} at these separations, monotonically in $q_1$ ($\chi^2=1249,1300,1357,1421$ for $q_1=0,0.3,0.6,0.9$ with the old projection), while $k_p$ is degenerate with the pixel window; this is consistent with the DESI DR1 full-shape analysis, which bounds $q_1<0.51$ at 95 per cent from the same data and leaves $\{k_v,a_v,b_v,k_p\}$ prior-dominated, and which fits the auto-correlation only at $r>25\Mpch$ \citep{2509.15308}. The lensing information is concentrated at small radial separations: 80 per cent of it comes from $r_\parallel<5\Mpch$ and half from $r_\perp<15\Mpch$ (Figure~\ref{fig:profile}).

\emph{How much freedom to give the correction.} The correction is fitted to the same pixel pairs that the estimator multiplies, so a random error $\delta g$ in the kernel attenuates the amplitude by $\langle W\,{\rm Var}(\delta g)\rangle/\langle Wg^2\rangle$: more parameters buy a better fit and cost response. Both sides are measurable. On DR1 the amplitude gain saturates almost at once --- $1.077$ with one cubic segment in each direction (16 coefficients for $S$ plus 6 for $N$), $1.099$ with 30, $1.100$ with 42 --- while the cost, measured on 40 mock realisations in Section~\ref{sec:mock}, does not: the paired response falls by $1.4\pm1.2$, $4.0\pm1.8$ and $10.4\pm2.3$ per cent for the same three. We therefore adopt the smallest, a bicubic surface in $(r_\perp,r_\parallel^2)$ with 22 fitted parameters in all, which takes $\chi^2$ from 1880 to 235 over the 810 cells and costs no measurable response.

\emph{Robustness.} With that choice the fitted kernel is insensitive to the parts of the construction that are choices rather than data. Across four continuum-projection variants (single full-slab forest; pair-weighted with the redshift-cut span; pair-weighted with the full picca span; an independent Monte-Carlo sample of the latter) the amplitude normalisation lies between $1.075$ and $1.079$ and the Fisher-weighted kernels agree to 2.0--5.3 per cent rms. We adopt the full-picca-span projection and quote $1.077$ for the normalisation change, with a 2 per cent systematic covering the knot and projection variations.

\subsection{Templates from low-redshift tracers}
\label{sec:templates}
For a tracer with objects at $(\vth_i,\chi_i)$ and mean density $\bar n(\chi)$ per steradian per unit distance, the kernel-weighted map
\begin{equation}
m(\vth)=\sum_{i\in{\rm pixel}}\frac{w_i\,W(\chi_i)}{b\,\bar n(\chi_i)\,\Omega_{\rm pix}}-\text{(randoms, scaled)},\qquad W(\chi)=\frac{3}{2}\Omega_m\frac{H_0^2}{c^2}(1+z)\chi\frac{\chi_s-\chi}{\chi_s},
\end{equation}
estimates the contribution of the tracer's slice to $\kl$ for a source at $\chi_s=\chi(z_{\rm eff})$, $z_{\rm eff}=2.348$, once the linear bias $b$ of the tracer is known: $\langle m\rangle=\int_{\rm slice}W\delta\,d\chi$. The randoms define the footprint (pixels holding at least half the mean random count, which excludes partially covered rim pixels), the completeness (their density smoothed by $1^\circ$) and the mean-density subtraction; the map is built at nside 512. The BOSS map is built the same way from its own randoms.

\paragraph{Bias from the angular power spectrum.} The bias of every (tracer, slice) is measured from its own map with NaMaster \citep{2019MNRAS.484.4127A}: the cross-spectrum of two random halves of the catalogue (which has no shot noise) is decoupled from the binary mask in bandpowers of width 40 and fitted, over $40\le\ell\le0.2\,\chi(z_{\rm mid})$ ($k\le0.2\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$) and with the NaMaster Gaussian covariance, against the Limber spectrum of the slice, $C_\ell=b^2\int_{\rm slice}W^2P(k=(\ell+\tfrac12)/\chi,\chi)\,d\chi/\chi^2$, times the pixel window and pushed through the same bandpower windows. This replaces the $f_{\rm sky}$-scaled pseudo-spectra and the 16-realisation Monte-Carlo mask transfer of the first version (which was 0.73--0.95 over the band for the fragmented DR1 footprint); the biases move by 1--3 per cent for the LRGs and ELGs and by up to 25 per cent for the noisiest quasar slice, where the covariance now weights the bandpowers properly. The shot noise of the full map (needed for the Wiener weights) is the white level of the half-difference map, measured as a pseudo-spectrum over $\langle M^2\rangle$: white pixel noise is local and that estimate is exact, whereas the NaMaster decoupling of a spectrum that is not band-limited attributes the coupling to $\ell>\ell_{\max}$ to the band and overshoots by 13--16 per cent for the DESI masks (2 per cent for BOSS). It agrees with the weighted Poisson expectation $\sum_i(u_iw_i)^2\Omega_{\rm pix}^2/\Omega_{\rm mask}$ to 1--3 per cent for the galaxy samples and to 10 per cent for the quasars. Figure~\ref{fig:spectra} shows the spectra and Table~\ref{tab:bias} the biases.

\paragraph{Wiener combination and the deflection template.} Within a slice the tracer maps (in convergence units, $m/\hat b$) are combined with the weights $\bm w_\ell=\mathsf C_\ell^{-1}\bm s_\ell$ of the model covariance $\mathsf C_{kl}=S_\ell+N_{kl}$ ($S_\ell$ the slice's convergence spectrum, $N$ the shot-noise matrix): inverse-shot-noise weighting of the tracers, Wiener-suppressed where noise dominates. Two features of the enlarged tracer set are handled here. The footprints differ (a BOSS slice covers a sky fraction of 0.22--0.24 against 0.17 for the DR1 LRGs, and their union 0.29--0.31), so the weights are computed per \emph{coverage class}, the subset of tracers covering a pixel: inside each class the filtered sum is the conditional expectation of $\kl$ given the tracers actually present, which is what keeps the amplitude estimator of Section~\ref{sec:qe} normalised to $A=1$ without further calibration; the slice mask is the union of the tracer masks. And BOSS and DESI share objects (34 per cent of the BOSS galaxies at $0.1$--$0.6$ are also BGS or LRG targets, 25 per cent at $0.6$--$0.8$), so their shot noise is correlated: the off-diagonal $N_{kl}$ is computed from a 1-arcsec positional match as $\sum_{\rm shared}(u_kw_k)(u_lw_l)\Omega_{\rm pix}^2/\Omega_{\rm joint}$, a noise correlation of 0.34 for BGS$\times$BOSS and LRG$\times$BOSS at $z<0.6$ and 0.11 at $0.6$--$0.8$ (the DESI tracers share fewer than $10^{-3}$ of their objects). The slices are independent, so the sum of the filtered slice maps is the conditional expectation of $\kl$ given all tracers; its deflection, $\bm\alpha^T=\nabla\phi$ with $\phi_{LM}=2\kappa_{LM}/[L(L+1)]$, evaluated at the sightlines (\texttt{healpy} derivatives at nside 1024), is the combined template. Each slice is also fitted on its own and the six slice amplitudes are combined with their joint jackknife covariance as a cross-check.

\paragraph{Validation of the deflection templates against CMB lensing.} The product the estimator consumes is the band-filtered deflection of the Wiener-filtered template. That field is built on the sphere for every science band and for the whole science window ($40\le L\le500$), and cross-correlated as a spin-1 field with the ACT and Planck convergence maps with NaMaster (spin-1$\times$spin-0 bandpowers of width 40, decoupled from the tracer mask and the CMB mask; the ACT map is treated as already carrying its mask squared, as the release prescribes; errors from the Gaussian covariance with the measured auto- and cross-spectra): the E-mode of the deflection carries the signal and its B-mode is mask leakage of the E-mode, which is quoted as the ratio B/E. The prediction is not $C_L^{\kl\kc}$: the template estimates the part of $\kl$ traced by objects at $z<1.75$, Wiener-suppressed where they are noisy, so for $T=\sum_s\sum_c\mathbb 1_c\sum_{k\in c}W^c_k\ast m_k$ the expectation of a pseudo-spectrum against $\kc$ on the sky weight $M$ (the product of the two masks) is $\langle T\kc\rangle_L=\sum_s\sum_c\frac{\langle\mathbb 1_cM\rangle}{\langle M\rangle}\sum_{k\in c}W^c_k(L)\,C_L^{(\ell_s,c)}p_L$, with $C^{(\ell_s,c)}$ the Limber cross-spectrum of the slice's contribution to $\kl$ with $\kc$ and $p_L$ the pixel window; the class fractions are taken inside the overlap with each CMB map and normalised by the overlap area, because NaMaster attributes the cross-spectrum to the whole field mask while a slice's tracers may cover only part of it (the slices above $z=0.8$ are DESI-only, the combined footprint includes BOSS-only sky). The deflection E-mode then reads $C_L^{E\kappa}=2/\sqrt{L(L+1)}\,F(L)\,\langle T\kc\rangle_L$ for a band window $F$. The amplitude $A_L$ of the measured cross relative to this prediction is expected to be one when the template chain (biases, shot noise, Wiener weights) is right, whatever the source redshifts; the ratio to the full $\kl\times\kc$ spectrum, which is what a naive comparison would use, is 0.16--0.57 depending on the band and is tabulated for reference. Table~\ref{tab:deflection} and Figure~\ref{fig:tcc} give the result (\texttt{report/stageb/deflection\_cmb\_check.json}, \texttt{cmb\_map\_checks.json}).

\subsection{Why not a CMB-lensing template}
The same estimator cross-correlated with $\kc$ measures lensing plus the intrinsic forest--forest--density three-point function of the forest's own redshift range. Its linear part, the response of the small-scale forest power to long-wavelength modes, can be removed with a kernel-matched template built from the $z>2$ quasars (the ``deprojection'' of the main report), but the second-order part, $\langle m_pm_qT\rangle$ with $m$ the long-wavelength component of the forest and $T$ a non-Gaussian tracer template, cannot be removed by one coefficient: on 400 mock realisations it biased the deprojected amplitude by $-0.41\pm0.18$ with no lensing and no response present. The lensing signal, the response and this term all act through the same long modes at the pair separations used, so the term has to be modelled, not filtered. A tracer at $z<1.8$ shares no density modes with the forest at $z>2.1$: its correlation with the pair products is lensing only, plus the magnification of the sightline quasars (absorbed by the mean field) and shared sky systematics.

\section{Validation on mocks}
\label{sec:mock}
The mocks are flat-sky FFT realisations of a $20^\circ\times20^\circ$ patch: a Gaussian matter field on a 2\,$\Mpch$ grid over the forest box $2.1<z<3.0$ (plus margins) from which the forest is a linear, redshift-space-distorted filter with $b_F=-0.13$, $\beta_F=1.6$ and a small-scale cut-off; 22 sightlines per deg$^2$ with pixel noise $P_N=0.33\Mpch$ (log-normal scatter across forests) and per-forest continuum projection; the ACT DR6 mask cut-out and a smooth completeness pattern; lognormal quasars behind the slab draw the sightlines. The foreground of $\kl$ (and of $\kc$) is generated in the five tracer slices as Gaussian projected-density maps with the exact Limber covariance between the density and the two convergence contributions, plus the uncovered rest; each slice hosts 2-D lognormal Poisson tracers of the projected density smoothed transversely by 3\,$\Mpch$ (a lognormal of the raw 0.7\,$\Mpch$ cells is absurdly non-Gaussian), with DR1-like biases and densities. The forest is lensed by the full $\kl$ (sampling at the displaced positions), and every realisation carries both a lensed and an unlensed forest.

The protocol (\texttt{GATES.md} v7) was frozen before the scale-1 campaign: 400 fresh realisations, templates built from each realisation's own tracers exactly as on the data (bias from the auto-spectrum, Wiener combination, sum over slices), and the gate ``an unbiased detection against a lognormal redshift tracer''. Figure~\ref{fig:mock} and Table~\ref{tab:mock} give the result: the combined null and recovery are unbiased, the paired response (the same realisation lensed and unlensed, free of sample variance) is $0.966\pm0.025$, equal to the response of the truth template ($0.970\pm0.018$; the 3 per cent deficit of the noisy sparse sample was already seen with the CMB template and is a property of the noisy forest, not of the template chain), the per-slice responses are unity within errors, a template from another realisation gives zero, the curl is zero, the jackknife error matches the ensemble scatter, and the noise-free injection expectation (the bookkeeping test of signs, cuts and band basis) has odd slope $0.98$. The only rows of the protocol that fail are the tracer-bias precision rows: the auto-spectrum bias of a lognormal tracer differs from the generator's by 1--3 per cent, resolved at $N=400$ against a 2-SEM rule that amounts to 0.4 per cent; the effect on $A$ is the same 1--3 per cent per slice.

\section{The quasar--forest cross-correlation}
\label{sec:cross}
The lensing information in the forest is not confined to the forest auto-correlation. For a quasar at $(\vth_q,\chi_q)$ and a forest pixel on another sightline at $(\vth_p,\chi_p)$, lensing remaps both positions, so the observed cross-correlation is $\xi_{qF}$ at the true separation,
\begin{equation}
\langle\dF(p)\,|\,\text{quasar at }q\rangle=\xi_{qF}(r_\perp,r_\parallel)+\frac{\partial\xi_{qF}}{\partial r_\perp}\,\chi\,\hat\vth_{qp}\cdot(\bm\alpha_q-\bm\alpha_p),
\label{eq:cross}
\end{equation}
the pair form of Equation~\eqref{eq:pair} with $(a,b)\to(q,p)$; the estimator of Section~\ref{sec:qe} applies with the quasar as the first member of every pair, $w_q=1$, and the product $\dF(p)\dF(q)$ replaced by $\dF(p)$. Magnification bias modulates the observed quasar density by $(5s-2)\kappa$, but the mean field is subtracted per \emph{observed} quasar, so that modulation contributes nothing at linear order in a foreground template (its residual, $(5s-2)^2\langle\kappa_{\rm fg}\kappa^T\rangle\,{\rm Cov}(\kappa_{\rm fz},\dF)$, is second order) and is dropped. The quasars are the DR1 quasar catalogue at $1.9<z<3.1$, one entry per target (583\,148 quasars, 377\,715 of them the quasars of forests in the sample); a quasar's own sightline is excluded from its pairs; the templates are evaluated at the quasar positions as at the sightlines, and the jackknife, curl and random templates are as before. The radial separation is signed, $r_\parallel=\chi_p-\chi_q$.

\paragraph{The cross-correlation model.} $\xi_{qF}$ is measured in $1\Mpch$ cells over $r_\perp<40$, $|r_\parallel|<40\Mpch$ and seven bins of the pair mean redshift ($3.6\times10^7$ quasar--sightline pairs), and fitted with an independent model built like Equation~\eqref{eq:xifit}:
\begin{multline}
\xi_{qF}(r_\perp,r_\parallel,z)=b_q(z)\,b_F(z)\Big[\tfrac{D(z)}{D(z_{\rm ref})}\Big]^2\big[T_0+(\beta_q+\beta_F)T_1+\beta_q\beta_FT_2\big](r_\perp,r_\parallel-\Delta r_\parallel)\otimes\mathcal N(\sigma_\parallel)\\+x^{\gamma_S}\sigma(r)S(r_\perp,r_\parallel),\qquad b_q(z)=b_q\,x^{\gamma_q},\quad\beta_q(z)=\frac{f(z)}{b_q(z)},
\label{eq:xiqf}
\end{multline}
where the basis tables are the same three Hankel transforms pushed through the DESI pixel windows and the \emph{one-sided} continuum projection (the projector acts on the forest pixels only, averaged over 400 real forests with the quasar distance stepped across and beyond each forest), the forest side ($b_F=-\sqrt{b_F^2}$, $\beta_F$ and their power laws) is held at the auto-correlation base fit, the quasar bias and its evolution are free with $\beta_q$ set by the growth rate of the cosmology, $\Delta r_\parallel$ is the quasar redshift offset (the observed correlation is the true one displaced along the line of sight) and $\sigma_\parallel$ a Gaussian redshift-error smoothing, both applied to the fine tables before the cell averaging, and the spline correction allows odd terms in $r_\parallel$. The base model fits with $b_q=3.20$ at $z_{\rm ref}=2.4$ evolving as $(1+z)^{1.7}$ (2.6 at $z=2$, 4.2 at $z=3$; the DESI quasar-clustering relation gives 3.8 at 2.4, 15 per cent higher, the difference being absorbed by the fixed $b_F$), $\beta_q(z_{\rm ref})=0.30$, $\Delta r_\parallel=0.44\Mpch$ and $\sigma_\parallel=4.9\Mpch$, with $\chi^2=6915$ over 11\,340 cells; with the base parameters held there, the 20-coefficient correction takes $\chi^2$ to 5729, uniformly across the redshift bins (0.46--0.65 per cell), and the measured-to-model amplitude per redshift bin is within 4 per cent of one. Figures~\ref{fig:qsoxi} and \ref{fig:qsoxi2} are the QA plots: the model follows the line-of-sight profiles at every $r_\perp$, offset and smoothing included, and the residual map in the fit range is featureless.

\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\subsection{The adopted fiducial: measured-covariance Wiener weights, one joint response matrix, injection}
\label{sec:fiducial}
Two changes requested after the band-set study define the adopted fiducial analysis (products \texttt{lowz\_v4}, \texttt{dr1\_lowz\_v7d}, \texttt{dr1\_qso\_v1d}, \texttt{joint\_fit\_v4.json}, \texttt{deflection\_cmb\_check\_v4.json}, \texttt{injection\_v4.json}).

\paragraph{Measured-covariance Wiener weights.} The per-slice combination of Section~\ref{sec:templates} used the model covariance $\mathsf C_{kl}=S_\ell+N_{kl}$, a rank-one common signal plus the shot-noise matrix, which is what linear bias with independent shot noise predicts. The tracers of a slice trace the same density field with different stochasticity, scale-dependent bias and systematic power, so $\mathsf C_{kl}(\ell)$ is now \emph{measured}: the full set of NaMaster auto- and cross-spectra of the unit-bias maps on the footprint common to the tracers of the slice, in bandpowers of width 40, interpolated to every multipole, with the diagonal floored at half the model and the off-diagonals clipped inside the correlation bound; the weights $\mathsf C^{-1}\bm s$ are still computed per coverage class and the class weights are stored with the templates, so the prediction of the CMB validation uses exactly the weights the template used. The measured spectra lie 5--20 per cent above the model in the $0.6$--$0.8$ slice, where the weights come down accordingly. The deflection validation over the science window is $0.96\pm0.04$ (ACT) and $0.92\pm0.03$ (Planck); per band $0.90$, $1.09$, $1.02$, $1.00$, $1.45$ ($\pm0.05$--$0.17$) against ACT and $0.84$, $0.98$, $0.97$, $1.38$, $1.22$ ($\pm0.04$--$0.20$) against Planck, the same pattern as with the model weights. The less aggressive weights cost 5--7 per cent in the error of the amplitudes at unchanged central values: separately fitted, $0.40\pm0.43$ (auto, was $0.34\pm0.40$) and $0.87\pm0.47$ (cross, was $0.87\pm0.45$).

\paragraph{One joint response matrix.} The per-slice fits of the earlier sections hold an $11\times11$ response matrix per slice with the other slices absent from the model (the cross-talk caveat of Section~\ref{sec:caveats}). The fiducial now fits all slice templates at once: five slices, each with its five science bands, their five curl partners and the junk band, i.e.\ 55 components and a $55\times55$ matrix $F$ per statistic, accumulated per jackknife region (\texttt{code/pipeline/joint\_fit.py}; the partial sums replace the $(N_t,N_t,N_{\rm pair})$ array that made this impractical before). Figure~\ref{fig:response} shows $F$ normalised to unit diagonal. It is close to block-diagonal: the largest elements, up to $0.11$, connect neighbouring bands of the same slice through the cosine tapers, while between slices they never exceed $0.09$ with an rms of $0.008$. The science amplitude is the common amplitude of the 25 gradient components with the 30 nuisance amplitudes free; the same design gives the per-slice, per-band and curl amplitudes as sub-fits. With the joint matrix the fiducial reads
\begin{equation}
A=0.42\pm0.43\ \text{(forest$\times$forest)},\quad A=0.86\pm0.47\ \text{(quasar$\times$forest)},\quad A=0.61\pm0.33\ \text{(combined)},\qquad A<1.16\ \text{at 95 per cent},
\end{equation}
with joint curl amplitudes $-0.44\pm0.59$ and $-0.64\pm0.79$, a jackknife correlation of $0.06$ between the two statistics and the cross-correlation carrying 45 per cent of the weight. Restricting $F$ to its per-slice blocks (the ``block-diagonal'' variant, equivalent to the separate fits) gives $0.42\pm0.43$, $0.84\pm0.47$ and $0.61\pm0.33$, so the cross-talk is immaterial at this precision, as the leakage matrix of Section~\ref{sec:caveats} anticipated. Per slice (combined statistics) the joint amplitudes are $0.86\pm0.79$, $0.20\pm0.63$, $0.39\pm0.80$, $1.12\pm0.73$ and $0.27\pm1.11$ for the five slices; per band $0.19\pm0.47$, $0.70\pm0.68$, $2.18\pm0.99$, $1.80\pm1.40$ and $-0.87\pm1.90$. The random-template nulls of the adopted products are $-0.20\pm0.09$ (auto, twenty templates, scatter 0.39 against the RMS jackknife 0.45) and $0.18\pm0.15$ (cross, scatter 0.68 against 0.58).

\paragraph{Injection tests for both statistics.} \texttt{code/stageb/injection\_dr1.py} shifts every position (sightlines, and for the cross-correlation the quasars) by $-A\bm\alpha_{\rm inj}$ with $\bm\alpha_{\rm inj}$ the science-window deflection of the combined template and $A=\pm0.25,\pm0.5$, rebuilds the pairs with the production selection and re-runs the estimator; the odd (paired) slope $[\hat A(+a)-\hat A(-a)]/2a$ is the figure of merit. In expectation mode ($\dF\dF$ or $\dF$ replaced by the fitted correlation at the true separation: the bookkeeping test of signs, local metric, cuts and band basis) the slopes are $1.025$ (auto, identical at both amplitudes; the 2.5 per cent is the displaced same-wavelength term discussed in Section~\ref{sec:result}) and $0.998$ (cross). \section{Caveats and systematics}
\label{sec:caveats}
At an error of 0.47 on $A$, systematic effects of 20 per cent are still below the noise; the following are recorded for the full-DESI measurement, the template normalisation first.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Slice-to-slice cross-talk.} Each slice is fitted on its own, with an $11\times11$ response matrix and the other slices absent from the model; strictly the fit should be joint, one $(11\times5)^2$ matrix, because the slice templates are not orthogonal on the pair basis. The leakage was measured for the five-slice version in \texttt{report/stageb/slice\_crosstalk.json}: the response correlation between the common-science directions of two slices never exceeds $0.037$, and the leakage matrix $L_{ss'}=\partial\hat A_s/\partial A_{s'}$ has row sums $0.97,0.99,0.95,0.89,1.06$, so a separate per-slice fit returns between 0.89 and 1.06 when every slice truly has $A=1$. Correcting the slice amplitudes by $L^{-1}$ moves their jackknife combination from $0.353\pm0.625$ to $0.377\pm0.650$, a $0.04\sigma$ shift. The combined-template result, which is the number we quote, is a single template in a single joint band fit and carries no slice decomposition, so it is unaffected. The joint fit is now implemented (Section~\ref{sec:fiducial}, per-region accumulation) and is the fiducial; the block-diagonal restriction of the joint matrix reproduces the separate fits and differs from the joint fit by $0.01$ in the combined amplitude.
\item \emph{One response table for two pair types.} With the evolving table the two pair types differ by 10 per cent in amplitude at fixed redshift (Section~\ref{sec:qe}); by the same weight argument as before (A$\times$B carries 22 per cent of the correlation weight and 21 per cent of the Fisher weight) the bias on $A$ is at the 0.1 per cent level, with the shape difference not separately cancelled.
\item \emph{High-$L$ bands.} The ratio of the jackknife error to the Fisher scale grows from 1.17 to 1.29 when the bands are extended to $L=500$, so the two new bands scatter more than the Gaussian estimate predicts. They also carry the $+0.27$ shift in the central value. The tracer bias is measured only over $40\le\ell\le0.2\chi$, which for the nearest slice ends at $\ell\simeq260$, so the $300$--$500$ bands rely on extrapolating the bias; the ELG auto-spectrum already shows scale-dependent bias above $\ell\simeq300$.
\item \emph{Tracer biases and the template normalisation.} The auto-spectrum and CMB-cross biases agree to 10--15 per cent for most tracers, and the deflection validation gives $A_L=0.99\pm0.04$ (ACT) and $0.92\pm0.03$ (Planck) for the combined template, $0.80$--$1.00$ per slice, with a mild rise with $L$. A template normalisation error $f$ scales the lensing amplitude by $1/f$, so the 8 per cent Planck deficit, if real, is a $+8$ per cent question for the result, within the budget; the ELG of the $0.8$--$1.1$ slice (cross-to-auto ratio 0.65 against both maps) remain the clearest individual outlier.
\item \emph{Forest correlation model.} The correction of Equation~\eqref{eq:xifit} is empirical: it is fitted to the same pixel pairs that the estimator multiplies, so the kernel can follow noise in the measured correlation, which attenuates the amplitude. With the adopted 22 parameters the mock test bounds that at $1.4\pm1.2$ per cent, and the model dependence of the kernel across the projection variants is 2.0--5.3 per cent rms, or 2 per cent on $A$. Both will need re-examining for the full sample: the attenuation is a property of the fit, not of the sample size, and the natural fix is to estimate the covariance of the measured cells (a jackknife of the correlation measurement) and to subtract $\langle W\,{\rm Var}(\delta g)\rangle$ from the response matrix.
\item \emph{Continuum projection.} The projection is now averaged over real forest pairs, but it remains a model of picca's continuum fit as a per-forest weighted mean-and-slope removal, and it ignores the stacked-delta correction that picca applies across forests at fixed observed wavelength. With the corrected projection the model predicts an $r_\perp$ tilt over $3<r_\perp<30\Mpch$ that the data do not show; the empirical correction absorbs the difference, and the kernel is the same to 5 per cent whichever projection is used as the base, but the disagreement is not understood.
\item \emph{Mask coupling.} The tracer spectra and the CMB cross-spectra are NaMaster bandpowers with analytic mode coupling; the residual approximation is the Gaussian covariance and, for the deflection field, the fact that the deflection inside a fragmented mask is computed from the potential of the masked map (the nonlocal part of the gradient is missing near mask edges), which the per-band comparison with the convergence template bounds at a few per cent.
\item \emph{Magnification and selection.} The sightline quasars are magnified by the foreground; the mean field absorbs the resulting modulation of the pair density, as in the mocks, where the sightline draw carries the magnification.
\item \emph{Cross-correlation kernel.} The cross-correlation amplitude uses the kernel of the fixed-base fit; refitting with all parameters and the correction free (\texttt{report/stageb/dr1\_qso\_v1\_free.json}) gives $0.64\pm0.51$ against $0.61\pm0.52$, a $+0.03$ shift, 5 per cent of the amplitude's error. The mocks contain no quasar--forest cross-correlation; the cross-correlation estimator is validated by its nulls, by the consistency of the redshift and band splits with the auto-correlation, by the QA of the fit and, since Section~\ref{sec:fiducial}, by the injection test (expectation slope $0.998$, real-data slope $0.998$).
\item \emph{Not yet done.} The mock validation has not been repeated with region B, with the evolving correlation table or with the enlarged tracer set: the A$\times$B machinery and the layered table are covered by unit tests (\texttt{tests/test\_regions.py}, \texttt{tests/test\_zevol.py}) and by the internal consistency of the runs, not by an end-to-end mock. Tomographic sub-slabs of the forest, the propagation of the bias uncertainties into the error, the 10 per cent residual amplitude difference between the two pair types at fixed redshift, the mock validation of the $1.96$--$2.1$ extension (the mock box starts at $z=2.1$), and a mock for the quasar--forest cross-correlation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions}
A pair-based quadratic estimator of the forest's lensing deflection, projected on a template from DESI and BOSS galaxies and quasars at $0.1<z<1.6$, is unbiased on 400 self-lensed mocks with lognormal tracers and gives $A=0.35\pm0.47$ on every DR1 forest pixel below $z=3$ (effective redshift 2.35), using both delta regions, bands to $L=500$, a redshift-evolving response kernel and eleven tracer samples, with clean curl, random-template and injection tests; the quasar--forest cross-correlation, treated with the same estimator and an independent correlation model, gives $A=0.61\pm0.52$, nearly uncorrelated with the auto-correlation, and the combination $A=0.58\pm0.31$ with the fiducial band set $40\le L\le1000$ ($0.47\pm0.35$ with the narrow set of the mock validation, $0.65\pm0.31$ extended to $L=1300$); with the adopted measured-covariance Wiener weights and the joint response matrix over all slices (Section~\ref{sec:fiducial}) it is $A=0.61\pm0.33$ ($0.42\pm0.43$ and $0.86\pm0.47$ separately), $A<1.16$ at 95 per cent, and the injected template deflection is recovered with slope $1.06$ (auto) and $1.00$ (cross). The template-side biases are measured internally with NaMaster, and the deflection templates are validated against ACT and Planck lensing, where they come out at $0.99\pm0.04$ (ACT) and $0.92\pm0.03$ (Planck) of their nominal normalisation, the two maps agreeing on common sky. The combined precision corresponds to a $1.2\sigma$ deficit from the $\Lambda$CDM value, better than forecast for DR1; the same pipeline on the complete DESI sample (roughly twice the forests and deeper coverage at $z>2.5$) should reach $2$--$3\sigma$.
